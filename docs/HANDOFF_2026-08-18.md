by the venv's activate script. If pytest's `slow` tests fail with a TraCI
connect error on a fresh Linux box, install `libXrender` etc. first — a minimal
Ubuntu image doesn't have the X11 libs `eclipse-sumo`'s bundled binary links
against, even for headless (`--no-step-log`) runs.

## The state of the science — read before training anything

The corridor calibration went through two rounds of "the baseline was
measuring the wrong thing," both caught by calibrating before training, not
after:

1. **`max_pressure` wasn't max-pressure.** First version passed
   `outgoing_queue=0.0`, reducing it to "serve the longest incoming queue" — a
   purely local rule with no coordination signal at all. Fixed (`f3259ed5`):
   real per-movement upstream-minus-downstream pressure.
2. **`green_wave` wasn't a fixed-time plan.** It re-requested a phase every
   decision step; `sumo_rl.TrafficSignal.set_next_phase` treats a same-phase
   request as a no-op, so acceptance landed only every second step, the green
   duration was an artefact of `min_green` blocking, and the coordination
   offsets — the entire point of a green wave — were re-timed away by that
   blocking. Fixed (`10d9fce7`): an explicit function of simulation time,
   `corridor_control.fixed_time_phase` / `plan_phase_seconds`.

**The second fix flipped the conclusion the whole corridor extension is
premised on.** Ten seeds, ten floors, both scenarios, each controller at its
own best floor, paired per seed:

| scenario | green_wave (own best) | max_pressure (own best) | paired mp − gw |
|---|---|---|---|
| corridor_peak  | mg10, 13.46 ± 0.22 s | mg15, 19.43 ± 0.73 s | +5.97 ± 0.64 s, gw wins 10/10, t=+29.7 |
| corridor_tidal | mg10, 13.96 ± 0.34 s | mg15, 20.19 ± 1.31 s | +6.23 ± 1.23 s, gw wins 10/10, t=+16.1 |

A **correctly-timed fixed plan beats the reactive controller by ~6 s on every
seed, on both scenarios.** The previous number (max-pressure winning by 1.94 s)
was the stated gate for training IPPO at all — "this corridor rewards
adaptation, which the single intersection never did." It doesn't; that result
was max-pressure measured against a green wave that wasn't one.

Why the fixed plan wins: C1–C2–C3 are 200 m apart at 13.89 m/s, a 14.4 s
travel time, and the 5 s decision grid's closest phase duration to that is
15 s (`min_green=10`). The optimum sits *at* the grid's resolution limit — 10 s
and 20 s phases each cost ~5 s more delay per trip. `corridor_tidal` (built to
punish a fixed plan by reversing the dominant arterial direction at the
half-hour mark, same total 2100 veh/h as peak) doesn't rescue the reactive
story either: green_wave loses only 0.5 s going from peak to tidal, because at
a 30 s cycle it re-serves both directions often enough that the reversal
barely registers.

Full detail, including the mechanism trace and the earlier low-load tidal
build's own defect, is in the `10d9fce7` and `14c5b6fa` commit messages —
`git log -p` those two before assuming you know the whole story from this
summary. Superseded numbers are kept, not deleted, specifically so nothing
gets silently re-used:
`analysis/corridor_sweep_greenwave_nonplan_superseded.csv`,
`analysis/corridor_sweep_tidal_lowload_superseded.csv`.

Two things reported but *not* bugs — don't "fix" them:
- `max_pressure`'s floor response oscillates (22.0, 28.5, 20.2, 25.6, 33.4, 41.1
  s at mg 5/10/15/20/25/30 on tidal). This is real: neither controller acts on
  `min_green` directly, a change is honoured at the first decision step at or
  after `min_green + yellow_time`, so floors 5–30 collapse onto switching
  intervals 10–35 s — the curve is already at max resolution for
  `delta_time=5`. `analysis/corridor_sweep.py`'s report prints the
  floor→interval mapping and flags aliases; read it before treating any floor
  curve as continuous.
- The single-intersection peak/off-peak tables are untouched by any of this —
  different route files, different reward-tuning history, already-closed
  question. Don't re-open them because the corridor moved.

## The decision this leaves open

The corridor demonstrably rewards **coordination** — a fixed plan with correct
offsets already delivers it, at a bar (~13.5–14.0 s) that's ~30% below what
IPPO was going to be judged against. Before spending any local compute:

1. **Train IPPO against the corrected bar anyway, report the negative if it
   loses under the stop rule already in the plan.** Cheapest, and "a
   correctly-timed fixed plan beats both max-pressure and learned control on a
   coordinated 3-signal corridor" is a legitimate, publishable negative result
   on its own.
2. **Find demand that a fixed plan genuinely can't serve** before training
   anything. The tidal reversal was too slow to matter (30 s cycle re-serves
   both directions inside the half-hour window). Candidates worth trying:
   unequal cross-street demand per node (so no single offset suits all three
   signals), or an incident/blockage event mid-episode. This is scenario
   design, calibrated the same way peak/tidal were — sweep both references on
   it before touching IPPO — not a training task.
3. **Stop the corridor extension, consolidate the single-intersection
   thesis.** It's already complete and valid on its own terms
   ([[offpeak-hp-transfer-collapse]] in Claude's memory has the full state).

No default was chosen. Whoever picks this up next should choose explicitly,
write the choice down, and only then spend compute — that's the discipline
that caught both defects above before they reached a trained model.

## If IPPO/MAPPO training goes ahead (path 1)

The plan at `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-02-sp2-independent-marl.md` has
four known defects that must be corrected before any of its numbers are
trusted, regardless of which path is chosen:

1. It ranks on `system_mean_waiting_time` (in-network average) rather than
   delay per completed trip from tripinfo — the survivorship-biased metric
   this project already withdrew a headline over
   (`docs/FINDINGS_2026-08-12.md`).
2. It evaluates on a single seed (42) — use ≥10, as the corridor sweep does;
   the whole point of the Poisson-arrival fix was to make seeds mean
   something.
3. Its learning gate (`trained ≤ untrained + 1e-6`) passes a policy that
   barely moved. Validate `ppo_core.py`'s hand-rolled PPO against SB3 PPO on
   the single-intersection env at matched hyperparameters/budget first —
   that's the top technical risk in the stack, and it's untested.
4. It must pass `min_green` explicitly everywhere; never let it inherit a
   default. That's exactly the class of silent-parameter bug this whole
   calibration effort exists to catch.

Since this is now local-only, size the run to what the machine can actually
do — measure `agent-steps/s` for one short IPPO run before committing to a
step budget, the same way `docs/AWS_CLOUD_GUIDE.md` was never trusted for
sizing without a local timing probe first.
