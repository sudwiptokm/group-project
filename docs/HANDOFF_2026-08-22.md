## What happened: SP8 — does breaking the corridor's regular spacing let RL win?

Full detail: `docs/FINDINGS_2026-08-22-sp8-irregular-spacing.md`. SP4/SP6/SP7
all found `green_wave` beating `max_pressure`/`idqn` on `corridor.net.xml` —
three signals 200m/200m apart in a straight line, a geometry
`green_wave`'s single-offset-per-signal plan can solve almost exactly
(equal spacing means one offset serves both arterial directions at once).
SP8 asked whether that's a property of the *controller* or the *geometry*:
built `corridor_irregular.net.xml` (same topology, demand, and controllers;
spacing changed to 578m then 78m instead of 200m/200m) and re-ran the same
three controllers zero-shot (no retraining).

**Result: idqn beats green_wave for the first time in this project.**

| controller | regular net | irregular net | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| green_wave | 13.47s | 19.62s | +6.15s ± 0.26s |
| idqn | 16.56s | 18.48s | +1.92s ± 0.39s |
| max_pressure | 26.38s | 22.17s | −4.21s ± 4.46s |

Mechanistically clean: `green_wave` degrades most because its coordinating
assumption (one offset serves both directions) is exactly what asymmetric
spacing breaks; `max_pressure`/`idqn` don't depend on geometry-baked offsets
and are comparatively untouched (`max_pressure` even improves, though with
high variance). n=3 seeds, same caveat every SP here carries — idqn's win
margin (1.14s) is thin relative to noise floors seen elsewhere in this
project. Real, disclosed, first-of-its-kind signal — not yet load-bearing on
its own.

**Engineering note for anyone extending this:** `net_file` is now threaded
through `make_corridor_env`, `corridor_baseline.run()`/`green_wave_actions()`,
and `train_corridor_dqn.evaluate()`. `green_wave`'s signal positions/speed
are read dynamically from whatever net file is in use
(`corridor_baseline._green_wave_inputs`, `sumolib`-based) instead of the
`SIGNAL_POSITIONS`/`FREE_FLOW_SPEED` module constants that existed before
SP8 — those required hand-sync on any geometry change, which would have been
a real correctness risk with two network variants on disk. Non-default
`net_file` runs get a `_net<label>` filename fragment; `compare.py` does not
know this tag on purpose (see `analysis/irregular_net_compare.py`'s
docstring for why — it deliberately bypasses `compare.py`'s glob-based
aggregation rather than teach it a fourth tag dimension).

## Where this leaves the project

The standing "consolidate on green_wave" recommendation
(`docs/HANDOFF_2026-08-21.md`, reaffirmed after SP6/SP7) is **no longer
unconditional**. It held across ordinary demand, demand shifts, and a
mid-episode incident — three axes, all on `corridor.net.xml`'s specific
geometry. SP8 adds a fourth axis (network geometry) and it does not hold
there: on one irregular-spacing variant, idqn wins. The honest framing is
narrower than either "consolidate" or "keep training RL" — the fixed plan's
advantage looks like a property of *this corridor's specific geometry*, not
a general one. Whether that generalizes (wider seeds, a second irregular
variant, a grid topology per the earlier "make it more complex" brainstorm)
is the natural next question.

## What's still open, if anyone wants to keep pushing

2. A second irregular-geometry variant (or a grid topology —

   whether SP8's result is about *this* asymmetry or asymmetric spacing in

   magnitude-diverse training curriculum closes it. Needs new training.
4. Incident-aware IDQN retraining (SP7, unresolved, and now arguably more
   interesting given SP8) — deliberately deferred by SP7's own scope. Needs
   new training.
