## What happened: two more sub-projects, both landed clean after real fix loops

**SP6 — does IDQN's zero-shot policy generalize across demand shifts?**
Full detail: `docs/FINDINGS_2026-08-22-sp6-idqn-demand-shift.md`. Trained
once on `corridor_peak`, evaluated zero-shot (no retraining) on three shift
types against `green_wave`'s own gap on the same scenarios:

- `corridor_tidal` (reversed-direction demand) — gap **held**: +2.87s vs
  the in-distribution +3.09s gap.
- `corridor_skew` (uneven cross-street load) — gap **held**: +3.12s, as
  close to in-distribution as noise allows.
- `corridor_offpeak` (same shape, lower magnitude) — gap **widened
  materially**, more than tripling to +11.26s. IDQN overfit to
  `corridor_peak`'s specific demand *magnitude*, not just its shape.

Verdict: IDQN generalizes across structural demand shifts but not magnitude
shifts — a real, disclosed asymmetry, not a uniform "it generalizes" or "it
doesn't" story.

**SP7 — does reactive/learned control earn anything under a mid-episode
incident?** Full detail: `docs/FINDINGS_2026-08-22-sp7-corridor-incident.md`.
Built a mid-episode lane closure (15 minutes, one lane of the C1_C2
arterial, fixed and deterministic) and compared `green_wave`/`max_pressure`/
`idqn` by incident cost (Δ = incident delay − each seed's own no-incident
delay), not raw delay:

| controller | no-incident | incident | Δ (mean ± sd, n=3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| green_wave | 13.47s | 14.60s | +1.13s ± 0.13s |
| max_pressure | 26.38s | 27.26s | +0.87s ± 3.57s |
| idqn | 16.56s | 17.05s | +0.49s ± 0.21s |

By mean Δ, idqn's cost is smallest — nominally the "interesting" case per
the spec's own decision rule (smaller than both baselines', not just the
fixed plan). But: `max_pressure`'s Δ is not one consistent number — it
sign-flips per seed (seed 42 gets *faster* under the incident, seed 43 gets
21% worse), a real seed-dependent effect, not noise. And idqn's smallest-Δ
result comes with the standard ceiling-effect caveat: it starts from a
worse absolute baseline than `green_wave` and has less room left to lose.
Verdict: doesn't change the standing consolidation recommendation, but is
a legitimate follow-up signal (incident-aware retraining) rather than a
third flat disconfirmation.

**Both sub-projects hit real bugs during execution, not just design
choices — worth knowing if you extend either:**
- SP6's final review caught a filename-glob confound in `compare.py`
  (zero-shot `_on_<scenario>`-tagged runs silently averaging into
  in-distribution rows) and fixed it with `_warn_mixed_eval_scenarios`.
- SP7's Task 6 run hit a real anomaly that led to finding two defects: the
  incident's lane closure only blocked the `passenger` vClass (84.5% of
  demand — `moto`/`auto` — ignored the "closed" lane entirely), and the
  comparison script subtracted a 10-seed baseline mean instead of each
  seed's own value, which would have been actively misleading given
  `max_pressure`'s confirmed-bimodal no-incident distribution at
  `min_green=10`. Both fixed, eval re-run, numbers changed materially — see
  the findings doc for the full before/after.
- SP7's own final review then caught the *same* glob-confound bug class
  recurring for its new `_incident` filename fragment (`compare.py` was
  silently reporting 100% incident runs as the plain `green_wave`
  baseline) and fixed it the same way, plus three more real issues (idqn's
  baseline wasn't seed-matched even though the data existed on disk, a
  regression test was vacuous, the findings doc didn't disclose that its
  whole-episode Δ dilutes the true in-window effect ~4x). All fixed,
  re-reviewed clean.

## Where this leaves the project

disruption) have tested reactive/learned control against a competently-timed

## What's still open, if anyone wants to keep pushing (not recommended by default)

From SP6/SP7's own "what this doesn't answer" sections:

1. `corridor_offpeak`'s gap-widening (SP6) is a real, disclosed asymmetry
   in what "generalization" means for this checkpoint — untested whether
   training on a magnitude-diverse curriculum (not just `corridor_peak`)
   would close it.
2. SP7's incident eval never produced the incident-*window* number the
   spec's own scope section asked for (only whole-episode delta shipped) —
   the true in-window effect is roughly 4x the reported headline. A
   follow-up would need per-trip depart/arrival filtering against the
   incident window, which doesn't exist yet.
3. SP7's n=3 seeds is the plan's designed sample size, not widened after
   seeing `max_pressure`'s seed-dependent sign flip — a 10-seed run (data
   for 7 more `max_pressure` seeds already exists in
   `analysis/corridor_sweep.csv`; only the incident-condition runs would
   need extending) would firm up whether that's signal or noise.
4. Incident-aware IDQN retraining (randomized timing/location/severity in
   training) is the natural next question if anyone wants to chase idqn's
   smallest-Δ result specifically — deliberately deferred by SP7's own
   scope, zero-shot was the point.
5. `corridor_skew`'s known flaw (SP4: 600 veh/h never approaches the
   ~1800 veh/h phase-split capacity) is still unaddressed — carried over
   from the SP4 handoff, untouched by SP6/SP7.
